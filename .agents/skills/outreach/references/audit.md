# Cohort audit and learning

Record outcomes separately from sends, keyed by prospect ID: observed_at, accepted,
replied, relevant_reply, chat_agreed, chat_booked, feedback_summary, evidence_source.
Use true/false/unknown deliberately. An unobserved state is unknown, pending is not
rejection, and acceptance does not imply a response. Preserve original message/version.
Do not send audit follow-ups without authorization or copy full private threads to a sheet.

Audit at comparable ages (e.g. 7 and 14 days after send). Report the denominator and
coverage for every rate. Count accepted/sent, relevant replies/sent and booked chats/sent;
also show unknown outcomes so observed rates aren't mistaken for complete measurements.
Group by direct ownership evidence, task, role, company type, source and pitch version.
List sample sizes; avoid calling small differences statistically meaningful.

Learn from the conversations: recurring manual tasks, time burden, current workaround,
artifact needed, whether they can evaluate a solution, and sponsor/budget path. A friendly
connection with no relevant workflow is weaker than a detailed workflow conversation.
Compare responders with the full contacted cohort to avoid survivorship bias. Write
profile hypotheses, contradictory examples, candidate-selection changes and the next
experiment. Change selection first while keeping the approved message stable.

Each report ends with continue/pause, rationale, next cohort definition and next audit
date. No useful feedback yet means insufficient evidence, not proof of no pain. A poor
fit signal should pause sending while research improves; don't increase volume to
compensate. Retain a diverse exploration slice when a promising profile emerges.

Apply [learning.md](learning.md) to convert audit evidence into the next selection policy.
Save mature denominators, policy version, exploration allocation and selection artifact.
